## Overview

Mode shift to cycling is a policy objective in many countries. However, cycling infrastructure designs often fail to produce the joined-up, direct, and safe networks needed for trips to switch. Planned interventions rarely align with demand, creating disjointed networks and ineffective investment. This paper introduces a new ‘corenet’ method for developing core cycling networks that provides complete and connected infrastructure designs for places with or without existing cycling networks. The method demonstrates its application in the Network Planning Tool for Scotland (NPT) to support city-wide network designs and serves as the foundation for the Network Planning Workspace (NPW) tool, enabling local authorities to plan cycle networks through a structured, tiered approach.

## Background & Motivation

Evidence-based transport planning is essential for reducing carbon emissions, improving safety, and enabling sustainable mobility at scale.

Understanding cycling infrastructure, network planning, active travel is critical for advancing safe and sustainable transport systems. Despite prior work, significant gaps remain — particularly in how we translate research findings into scalable, real-world tools.

## Methodology

This section outlines our systematic approach to generating core cycling networks that integrate transport agency requirements with empirical cycling potential data, origin-destination travel patterns, and network topology. While the method uses graph theory concepts for network analysis, it is fundamentally driven by travel demand data and policy objectives rather than network structure alone.

The approach is designed to be reproducible and adaptable to different contexts, making the findings broadly applicable.

## Implications & Conclusions

The methods presented in this paper generate data-driven and scal­ able networks to support investment in the joined-up networks that are needed for cost-effective investment in cycling infrastructure. An important aspect is its flexibility: parameters determining the nature of the networks generated were adjusted following consultation with planners and the approach can be used in other contexts.

This paper makes a meaningful contribution to transport network planning, infrastructure assessment, and sustainable mobility and provides a solid foundation for future investigations. The methods and results can directly inform policy, design standards, or operational practice.
